Carpet Water Damage Restoration in Middletown, OH

Wet carpet can often be saved when it is extracted and dried fast. Call to find out what can stay and what should go.

Carpet has two parts that matter in a water loss: the carpet itself and the pad beneath it. Water goes through the carpet to the pad, which holds it like a sponge. The pad then keeps feeding moisture back up and into the subfloor.

Whether carpet can be saved depends on the water type, how long it sat and what the carpet is made of. Clean water that is removed quickly gives the best result.

When carpet is soaking wet

  • Lift furniture off the carpet and put foil or blocks under the legs.
  • Open windows or run the AC to move air, and avoid space heaters.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet.

Is My Wet Carpet Salvageable?

Carpet from a clean water source, such as a supply line, can usually be extracted, lifted and dried. The pad is more often replaced. Carpet touched by sewage or floodwater is generally discarded.

Time matters. After a couple of days, mold and odor start to appear and bonding agents weaken. A moisture check after extraction shows how much the carpet and subfloor hold.

Under ANSI/IICRC S500, carpet exposed to clean water can often be dried or reinstalled, while pad exposed to gray or Category 3 water is generally removed.

How We Restore Wet Carpet

Extraction

Weighted extraction tools pull water from carpet and pad. Large rooms use truck mounted or high power units.

Lifting and drying

The carpet is peeled back from the tack strip, the pad is removed if needed and air movers dry both sides. The subfloor is checked and treated.

Reinstalling

Dry carpet is stretched back into place on a new pad. A carpet installer may do this stretch. We hand off a clear record.

Replacing a pad is inexpensive compared with replacing carpet. A new pad also gives you a clean, dry base.

Odors, Stains and Deodorizing

Musty smell is a sign that moisture remains. Drying is the first step, followed by cleaning and, if needed, deodorizing. Masking an odor without drying only delays the problem.

Stains from dye bleeding, rust from metal furniture feet and tannin from wood furniture can show up as carpet dries. A cleaning pass after drying usually handles these.

Carpet Types and How They Handle Water

Nylon and polyester carpets handle water well, while wool takes longer to dry and can shrink. Natural backing in older carpet can break down. Berber loops can snag during lifting.

Knowing the type helps the crew choose the right tools and temperature. A quick test of dye stability can warn of bleeding. We tell you what we see before we make decisions.

Tack strips along the edges can rust and stain the carpet. They are checked and replaced when needed.

Wet Carpet on a Slab or Over Wood

Carpet over concrete behaves differently from carpet over a wood subfloor. Concrete holds moisture and releases it into the pad and carpet. Wood absorbs water and may swell.

Floor drying mats and dehumidifiers handle the slab. For wood subfloor, air movers and readings track the drying path. The pad is rarely reused in either case.

Check closet floors and corners. Water collects in low spots and under furniture where it is easy to miss.

What You Can Do While You Wait

  • Remove small items from the carpet and place them on a dry surface.
  • Put foil or plastic blocks under furniture legs.
  • Run the air conditioner or open windows to move air.
  • Avoid walking on wet carpet to prevent stretching.
  • Take photos of the wet area and the water source.

These steps cost nothing and help the crew start from a better place.
