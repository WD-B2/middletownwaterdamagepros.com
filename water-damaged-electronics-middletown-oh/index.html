Water-Damaged Electronics: When to Unplug in Middletown, OH

Water and electricity do not mix. This guide covers when to unplug, what to leave alone and when to call an electrician.

Published November 5, 2026 · Updated November 12, 2026

Electronics and water create two risks. One is shock or fire. The other is damage to the device. Both are reasons to slow down and avoid the urge to plug something in to see if it works.

This guide explains the safest order of operations when electronics get wet. It covers small devices, large appliances and the building electrical system.

Safety First: Power and Standing Water

If water is on the floor near outlets, power strips or appliances, do not step into it. Contact with water and live electricity can cause serious injury. Turn off power at the breaker only if you can reach it without standing in water.

If you cannot reach the panel safely, stay out and call your electric utility or an electrician. Do not touch wet cords or plugs.

ANSI/IICRC S500 lists electrical hazards among the first safety checks on a water job, and the same caution applies to anything plugged in at your house.

Small Electronics

Phones, laptops, tablets and TVs should be unplugged if it is safe, powered off and left alone. Do not try to turn them on. Remove batteries if you can. Wipe the outside gently.

Rice does not do much. The better step is to keep devices in a dry, ventilated place and have a repair shop open them. Water damage often causes corrosion that appears days later.

Steps for a wet device

  • Do not power it on.
  • Unplug and remove battery if possible.
  • Wipe the exterior gently.
  • Keep in a dry, ventilated place.
  • Take it to a repair shop soon.
  • Back up data once it is safe to do so.

Large Appliances and Outlets

Refrigerators, washers, dryers, ranges and water heaters should not be used after being in water until a qualified technician checks them. Water can damage motors, controls and wiring.

Outlets and switches that were wet should be checked by an electrician before use. Even if they seem to work, moisture inside can cause failure later.

The Electrical Panel and Wiring

A wet panel is an emergency. Call your utility or an electrician and stay away. Do not flip breakers if the panel is wet or the floor is wet.

Wiring inside wet walls can be damaged even if outlets seem fine. A restoration crew can map moisture and flag affected areas. An electrician decides what can be reused.

Data and Documents on Devices

If you have backups, you are in good shape. If not, a data recovery professional can sometimes recover data from damaged drives. Do not power on a wet drive. Keep it dry and call a specialist.

Take note of what was on the device and when you last backed up. This helps with recovery and with insurance.

Insurance and Replacement

Electronics fall under contents coverage in many policies. Take photos of damaged devices, including serial numbers and labels. Keep receipts if you have them.

Do not throw away devices until your insurer has had a chance to ask for them. A short list with brand, model and age helps.

Warning Signs That a Device Is Unsafe

  • A burning or chemical smell near a charger or outlet.
  • Sparks, popping sounds or a hot plug.
  • A breaker that trips again after you reset it.
  • A device that works briefly and then shuts off.
  • Corrosion or green residue on contacts.

If you see any of these, unplug the device if you can do so safely and stop using it. Do not use a charger that was wet. Water on a power adapter can create a shock or fire risk.

Call an electrician for anything connected to the building wiring.

Chargers, Batteries and Power Strips

Power strips and extension cords that were wet should be thrown away. Rechargeable batteries can short out when wet and may swell or overheat. Place them on a nonflammable surface away from other items and contact your local household hazardous waste program for disposal.

Do not charge a swollen or damaged battery. It can ignite.

Protecting Electronics Before the Next Event

Keep computers, routers and media equipment off the floor, especially in basements. Use shelves or wall mounts. Back up devices to the cloud so a loss does not wipe your files.

Install a battery backup and surge protector for sensitive equipment. A leak sensor near electronics can warn you before water reaches the cables.

Label your breaker panel clearly. In an emergency you should be able to find the right switch without hunting.

Recap: Safety Before Salvage

Your safety comes first, the device second, the data third. Unplug only when you can do it dry. Leave wet devices off. Let an electrician clear any outlet or panel that took on water before you restore power.

Back up what matters and keep your devices off the floor. Both habits save more money than any repair.

Middletown Note: Storm Outages and Surges

Storms in southwest Ohio can cause power outages and surges. When the power comes back on, wet equipment can fail dangerously. After a storm, check the basement and utility rooms for water before restoring power to a flooded area. If water reached outlets or appliances, wait for an electrician.

Use surge protectors for sensitive equipment, and keep devices off the basement floor. If your basement tends to take water, raise the furnace controls and electrical items where possible. That limits what a wet event can reach.
