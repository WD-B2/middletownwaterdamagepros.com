What to Keep or Toss After Water Damage in Middletown, OH

Not everything wet is ruined. The water type and the material decide what is worth saving.

Published October 22, 2026 · Updated November 12, 2026

After a water loss, the pile of wet belongings can feel overwhelming. You want to save what you can, but you do not want to keep something that is unsafe or will grow mold.

This guide gives you a simple way to sort. It starts with the water type, moves to the material and ends with a few practical tips for documenting what you discard.

Sort by Water Type First

Clean water from a supply line is much more forgiving than sewage or floodwater. If you are not sure of the source, assume the water was contaminated for soft goods.

With clean water, many items can be dried and cleaned. With contaminated water, porous items are usually discarded. Hard items can often be disinfected.

The keep or toss guidance here follows the water categories in ANSI/IICRC S500, where contaminated water changes the answer for porous items.

Food, Medicine and Cosmetics

Discard food that touched floodwater or sewage. Cans can be cleaned and sanitized if they are not dented or swollen. Throw away anything in porous packaging such as cardboard or plastic wrap that touched contaminated water.

Medicines and cosmetics that were in contact with water should be thrown out. Ask your pharmacist about replacing prescriptions.

Building Materials

Wet insulation and carpet pad are usually removed. Drywall that is soft or sagging is cut out. Firm drywall can often be dried in place if the water was clean. Wood framing is almost always kept, dried and checked.

Hardwood flooring is the hardest call. Some floors recover, and some do not. Read our hardwood floor page for details.

Photograph Before You Discard

Take photos of every item before you toss it. Write down a short description, its age and approximate value. Keep a few items such as a lamp base or a label if your insurer asks.

This list is the heart of a contents claim. Without it, memory fades and value is hard to prove.

Discard checklist

  • Photograph each item.
  • Write a short description and approximate value.
  • Place discards in heavy bags.
  • Keep items the adjuster asks to see.
  • Keep your list in a safe place.

Cleaning What You Keep

Hard goods can be washed with warm water and mild detergent, then sanitized with a diluted bleach solution if appropriate. Rinse and air dry. Do not mix cleaners.

Clothing should be washed as soon as possible. If you cannot wash it right away, rinse it and keep it damp in a bag rather than letting it dry stiff with mud.

Kitchen, Pantry and Medicine Cabinet Details

Food in sealed cans and jars can be saved if you wash and sanitize the outside. Throw away anything with a damaged seal or a swollen can. Discard food in cardboard, foil or plastic wrap that touched contaminated water.

In the bathroom, discard cosmetics and toiletries that were in contact with floodwater or sewage. Ask a pharmacist before using any medicine that got wet.

Tools, Toys and Decor

Metal tools can be washed, dried quickly and oiled to prevent rust. Plastic toys can be washed and sanitized. Stuffed animals and soft toys that touched contaminated water should be discarded.

Wooden decor may warp or stain. Dry it slowly and decide later whether it is worth refinishing. Framed art needs careful handling. Remove it from the frame gently and let it dry flat.

Hold onto sentimental items until you can decide calmly. If they are not a health risk, put them in a dry place and review them later.

Make a Simple Inventory

Make a list as you sort. Note the item, room, age and approximate value. Take one photo of each group. Keep the list in your phone and a copy in the cloud.

This inventory helps with insurance and with your own memory. A year from now you will not recall every item, and the list will.

  • Room and item description.
  • Brand or model if known.
  • Age and approximate value.
  • Photo reference.
  • Keep, clean or discard.

Recap: A Short Rule of Thumb

Hard and clean can usually stay. Soft and wet from contaminated water usually goes. Everything in between deserves a closer look and a photo before you decide. When in doubt about a health risk, discard it and write it down.

Sorting takes a day or two of steady work. Break it into rooms so it does not feel endless.

Middletown Note: Donation and Disposal Planning

After a larger loss, you may have a lot of debris. Check with your trash hauler or the City of Middletown about bulk pickup rules and limits before piling items on the curb. Planning disposal ahead keeps the cleanup moving and avoids fines or missed pickups.

Items that survive but you no longer need can be donated once they are clean and dry. Many local charities do not accept items touched by floodwater or sewage, so ask before you load a truck.
