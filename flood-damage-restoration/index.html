Floodwater picks up soil, fuel, fertilizer and sewage as it moves across the ground. Once it enters a home, it is treated as contaminated water, even if it looks muddy rather than foul.

Middletown sits on the Great Miami River, and river valleys carry a real history of flooding. After the 1913 floods, the Miami Conservancy District built dry dams upstream to protect cities like this one. Those dams do not stop every local water event, and heavy rain can still overwhelm drains, basements and low yards.

Before you enter a flooded space

  • Stay out if water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, and turn power off only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  • Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, and keep children and pets away from the water.
  • Photograph the water line and damaged items, then call the 24/7 line.

Emergency Flood Damage Cleanup: The Sequence

Safety comes first. Crews check for electrical hazards, structural damage and gas smells before work starts. Contaminated water means protective gear and controlled work areas.

Next comes water removal. Pumps clear deep water, extractors handle floors, and wet vacuums finish edges. Soaked carpet pad, insulation and low drywall are often removed because they hold contaminants.

After removal, surfaces are cleaned and treated, then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Final readings confirm the structure is dry before rebuild begins.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard classes floodwater and river water as Category 3, and that is the reason carpet, pad and low drywall are usually removed rather than cleaned.

Water Categories and What They Mean

How restoration crews describe water
CategoryTypical sourceHandling.
Category 1Broken supply line or clean rainfall indoorsExtraction and drying, most materials can stay.
Category 2Washing machine overflow or aged clean waterExtraction, cleaning and removal of porous items.
Category 3Floodwater, sewage or river waterProtective gear, removal of porous materials, disinfection.

Floodwater is generally treated as Category 3. That does not mean the house is a loss. It means porous materials such as carpet and pad usually come out, while structural wood can be cleaned and dried.

Flood Risk in the Great Miami River Corridor

Homes near the river, in low lots or below street grade deal with water differently. A basement may take water through floor drains, wall cracks or window wells long before the street floods. Knowing whether your address is in a mapped flood zone is worth the few minutes it takes.

The FEMA flood map service lets you look up an address. Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood, so many owners in river areas buy separate flood coverage. Ask your agent what yours includes before the next storm.

Contents, Documents and Photos After a Flood

Move what you can to dry, elevated areas, starting with documents, photos and electronics. Do not run wet electronics. Hard goods like dishes and tools can usually be cleaned. Soft goods like mattresses and upholstered furniture touched by floodwater are often discarded.

Keep a list as you go. Write down what was lost, its approximate age and where it was in the house. A clear list makes insurance conversations easier.

Safety Notes for Flooded Homes

Electricity and water do not mix. If water reached outlets, appliances or the panel, wait for a qualified electrician before restoring power. Do not use a gas appliance that was in water until it has been inspected.

Floodwater can hide sharp debris, broken glass and slick mud. Wear sturdy boots and gloves. Keep children and pets out of the area until cleanup is complete.

Do not mix household cleaners, since some combinations release harmful fumes. Plain soap and water is a safe first step for hard surfaces while you wait for the crew.

What Recovery Looks Like Over the First Week

  • Day one covers safety checks, extraction and removal of porous materials that cannot be saved.
  • Days two to four focus on controlled drying with air movers and dehumidifiers, plus daily meter checks.
  • Day five onward confirms dry readings, cleans surfaces and prepares a rebuild scope.
  • Rebuild begins only after the structure meets drying goals.

Timelines shift with depth, materials and weather. Each step is logged so you and your adjuster can see progress.
