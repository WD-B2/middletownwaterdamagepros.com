Sherman is a residential neighborhood in Middletown, about a mile from downtown. A Homes.com neighborhood guide lists a median build year of 1915 for the area and describes small bungalows and Foursquares on compact lots. Sherman Park sits on Sherman Avenue, and a Journal-News report on the park describes it as surrounded by dense housing.

Compact homes shape how water damage plays out. A leak in one room has a short path to the next. This page explains what that means for extraction, drying and the order of the first steps. If water is moving now, call (844) 950-6900.

Where Sherman Is

Sherman Avenue and Sherman Park give the neighborhood its name and its center. Central Avenue is an anchor on the northern side, and Roosevelt Boulevard and University Boulevard are mentioned as nearby streets. We describe the area approximately and do not claim official boundaries.

Sunset Park lies just outside the neighborhood according to the same guide. If your home is near the edge, call with your address and we will confirm it.

Why Small Homes Need a Fast Plan

With less floor area, water can reach walls, closets and the next room quickly. The first hour matters most for stopping the source and moving belongings. After that, the goal is to remove water and start drying before wet materials stay wet.

Tight rooms also mean equipment placement takes thought. Air movers and dehumidifiers need airflow, and a crew will often use hallways and doors to set up a drying path. Ask where equipment will go so you can plan how to live in the house.

If Water Is Spreading Through a Sherman Home

Stop the source

Turn off the fixture, appliance or main shutoff only if you can reach it while staying dry.

Check for electric hazards

Stay away from wet outlets and panels. Do not use fans or vacuums in standing water.

Lift and photograph

Move small items, rugs and papers to dry spots. Photograph each wet room when it is safe.

Call us

River and Storm Context for Middletown

The Great Miami River runs through Middletown. The Miami Conservancy District reports that it maintains 9.6 miles of levee and 8.4 miles of river channel in the city. A Purdue climate center account of the 1913 flood says more than a quarter of the city flooded, including the business district and a large residential section.

That is citywide history, not a report on Sherman. We have not found evidence of how a particular Sherman street fared, and we do not claim any. In small homes, the laundry, kitchen and bath are often close together. A single washing machine hose can wet more than one room. Know where each shutoff is before you need it.
