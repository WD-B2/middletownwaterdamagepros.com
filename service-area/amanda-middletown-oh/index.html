Septic Systems and Contaminated Water

Soapbox Middletown reported that many Amanda residents use septic tanks, and that the neighborhood was annexed to the city in the 1990s. Not every home is on septic, and we cannot say which are. Ask your landlord, your neighbors or the county if you are unsure.

A failing septic system or a blocked sewer line can send wastewater into a home or yard. That water is contaminated. It is not something to mop up with towels or run fans over.

Wastewater is a health hazard

Keep people and pets away from sewage or drain backups. Do not use household fans, ordinary vacuums or DIY drying on it. Porous materials such as carpet and padding that touch it are usually removed, not dried. Call a professional crew.

Our sewage backup page explains containment, removal, cleaning and disinfection. The plumber or septic contractor fixes the system itself, and the restoration crew handles the damage it caused.

If Wastewater or Storm Water Is Inside an Amanda Home

Keep everyone out

Move people and pets away from the affected room. Do not walk through the water.

Avoid electrical hazards

Stay away from wet outlets, cords and panels. Do not run equipment in standing water.

Do not flush or run drains

Stop using sinks, tubs and toilets if a backup is possible. That keeps more water from entering.

Single Story Homes and Slab or Crawl Space Questions

The guide describes many homes as compact single story houses with a median build year of 1951. It does not say whether they sit on basements, crawl spaces or slabs, and we do not assume it. Check your own home and tell the crew.

In a single story home, water often spreads across one level. Flooring, baseboards and lower walls take the first exposure. Moisture readings show how far it went, and the plan follows from them.

Storm Water and Heavy Rain

Heavy rain can raise water at doors, windows and low yards. It can also overload drains. The first steps are to stay out of any water that may be near live electrical equipment and to photograph the damage.

See our storm cleanup page and flood damage page for the process. Water that came from outside or from a drain is treated as contaminated until a crew assesses it.

Drying and Documentation

After removal and cleaning, drying uses air movers and dehumidifiers with recorded readings. Professional crews commonly use the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as a reference. Our structural drying page explains the steps.

Photograph the damage and keep notes. Coverage depends on the cause and your policy, and some policies treat sewer backup differently from other water. Ask your insurer or agent what applies.
